An App-First Offline Messaging Protocol — What Spectrum Can We Actually Use?

Core product = a mobile/laptop app. So the real constraint is: what can software drive on an unmodified device?

Then, a deep, honest map of every other physical channel — from sound and light to neutrinos.

Design note · 23 Jul 2026
The one idea that unlocks everything: an app can't magically use LoRa or ham frequencies — it's limited to the radios and transducers already in the phone. But two of those transducers — the speaker/microphone and the screen/camera/flashlight — let an app transmit over sound and light. Those are genuinely "other spectrums," they need no extra hardware, and they're completely unregulated because they aren't radio. That's your creative edge, and it's buildable today.

1 · Why the app is the constraint (and why that's fine)

A modern phone contains many radios, but the operating system only exposes a few to apps, and each phone's radios are type-certified for specific bands only — you cannot legally or technically make an app transmit on the FM chip, cellular bands, or arbitrary "scientific" frequencies. What an app can freely command splits into two families:

  • Exposed radios: Bluetooth LE, Wi-Fi (Direct + Wi-Fi Aware/NAN), Ultra-Wideband (on newer phones), and NFC. All operate in license-free ISM bands — no permission needed.
  • Transducers (the clever part): the speaker + microphone (acoustic / near-ultrasonic data) and the screen + camera + flashlight LED (optical data). These move information through air pressure waves and visible light — physics channels that no spectrum regulator controls.

So the honest boundary is: the app itself lives entirely in Tier 1 below. Everything more exotic — LoRa, HF, meteor scatter, lasers — reaches the network only through a gateway node the app talks to. Designing for that split is the whole trick.

On "astrophysics lab relays" specifically: radio telescopes and the Deep Space Network are essentially receive-only, single-owner scientific instruments — they don't relay third-party traffic, and you can't legally transmit into their bands (radio-astronomy spectrum is protected from emissions, the opposite of open). The usable "science-adjacent" relays that do exist are amateur ones: ham satellites (AMSAT), the ISS APRS digipeater, and high-altitude balloons — all real, all requiring a licence, all better treated as optional gateways.

3 · The protocol: bind to no single transport

The mistake would be to build "a Bluetooth messenger." Build a transport-abstraction protocol where a message is independent of how it travels — so the same encrypted packet can hop over BLE now, sound across a noisy room next, Wi-Fi Aware to the next cluster, and out through a LoRa or internet gateway when one appears. This is exactly what Reticulum and libp2p already model, so you'd extend, not invent.

App / UX
Your mobile + laptop client — contacts, messages, alerts, gateway status
Identity
Cryptographic address = public key (no phone numbers, no servers)
Store & forward
Delay-tolerant delivery — hold the message, forward when any path opens (Bundle Protocol / LXMF model)
Routing
Self-configuring, transport-agnostic (Reticulum / Yggdrasil style)
Transport interfaces
Tier 1 in the app: BLE · Wi-Fi Aware · UWB · ultrasound (ggwave) · optical (streaming QR / flashlight). Via gateways: LoRa, ham, SDR, FSO.
Gateways
Any node with a peripheral or internet uplink bridges the offline mesh to long-range + the web
Why sound + light matter so much to an app-first product: they're the only extra "spectrums" you can ship in a pure software update, with no dongle and no licence. They let two phones talk when Bluetooth is jammed or disabled, bridge a phone to a laptop or a cheap microcontroller, and even work across an air gap or a window. Put an ultrasound interface (ggwave/Quiet) and an optical interface (streaming QR + flashlight) in the protocol as first-class fallbacks alongside BLE/Wi-Fi. Nobody else's disaster app does this well — it's a real differentiator.

4 · Recommendation

  • Core app transports: BLE mesh (primary) + Wi-Fi Aware (bandwidth) + ultrasound + optical (fallbacks). All Tier 1, all license-free, all pure software.
  • Protocol base: prototype on Reticulum (transport-agnostic, crypto addressing, self-routing, has a delay-tolerant messaging layer) — add sound/light as custom interfaces. It's the least reinvention for the most of your idea.
  • Long range: ship an optional LoRa gateway (Meshtastic-compatible, 865–867 MHz in India) that the app auto-discovers — that's your km-scale reach without breaking the "it's just an app" model.
  • Exotic physics: treat HF/meteor-scatter/EME/FSO as specialist gateway sites for a later phase, and file neutrino/quantum under "inspiring, not shipping."
Two honest caveats. (1) Regulation follows the emission, not the intent — a frequency being "scientific" doesn't make it free; deliberate RF transmission is licensed regardless, and radio-astronomy bands are actively protected from transmitters. The only truly open lanes are the ISM/license-free radios your phone already uses, plus non-RF physics (sound, light). (2) iOS restricts background BLE/Wi-Fi and peer APIs more than Android — validate the transport set on both platforms early. India spectrum rules from the earlier docs still apply to any radio layer.
