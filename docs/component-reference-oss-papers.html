Component Reference — Open-Source Implementations & Research

For each layer of the network design: what to reuse (OSS) and what to read (papers) for authenticated "what-to-improve" feedback.

Companion to the network-layer design · 23 Jul 2026
Each block below maps to a layer of the network-layer design. Green = code you can build on. Amber = the papers, each with a one-line note on what it validates or where it can be pushed further. The security-analysis section at the end is the most important for you — it's peer-reviewed evidence of how these exact systems fail in the field.

Layer 0–1 Transports & interface abstraction

Open-source

Transport-agnostic stack — markqvist/Reticulum · LoRa mesh — meshtastic/firmware · data-over-sound — ggerganov/ggwave, quiet/quiet · solar LoRa node — sudomesh/disaster-radio

Research

Nandakumar, Chintalapudi, Padmanabhan, Venkatesan. "Dhwani: Secure Peer-to-Peer Acoustic NFC."
ACM SIGCOMM 2013.
Use for: the acoustic (speaker↔mic) channel — proves secure data-over-sound is real. Improve: range & noise robustness in a chaotic disaster soundscape.

Layer 2 Identity & end-to-end encryption

Open-source

Signal protocol — signalapp/libsignal · Matrix E2E — Olm/Megolm · handshake framework — Noise Protocol · crypto primitives — libsodium · file/stream encryption — FiloSottile/age

Research

Cohn-Gordon, Cremers, Dowling, Garratt, Stebila. "A Formal Security Analysis of the Signal Messaging Protocol."
IEEE EuroS&P 2017 (extended in J. Cryptology 2020).
Use for: confidence the Double Ratchet's guarantees are formally proven. Improve: its assumptions break under long one-way delays — check them against your DTN worst case.
PDF
Alwen, Coretti, Dodis. "The Double Ratchet: Security Notions, Proofs, and Modularization for the Signal Protocol."
EUROCRYPT 2019.
Use for: a cleaner, modular ratchet you could adapt for store-and-forward instead of copying Signal wholesale.
PDF

Layer 3 Peer discovery & naming

Open-source

Research

Maymounkov, Mazières. "Kademlia: A Peer-to-Peer Information System Based on the XOR Metric."
IPTPS 2002.
Use for: the discovery layer when nodes are online. Improve: DHTs are weak to Sybil/eclipse attacks and heavy churn — both are worse in a disaster; pair with gossip + reputation (Layer 6).

Layer 4 Delay-tolerant routing & delivery

Open-source

Bundle Protocol 7 — dtn7/dtn7-go · NASA reference — nasa-jpl/ION-DTN · IBR-DTN · Reticulum routing

Research

Fall. "A Delay-Tolerant Network Architecture for Challenged Internets."
ACM SIGCOMM 2003.
Use for: the founding blueprint of your whole store-and-forward model (bundles, custody).
PDF
Vahdat, Becker. "Epidemic Routing for Partially-Connected Ad Hoc Networks."
Duke Univ. Tech Report CS-2000-06, 2000.
Use for: the baseline "flood copies for reliability" idea. Improve: it's buffer- and battery-hungry — the reason you constrain it with spray-and-wait.
PDF
Spyropoulos, Psounis, Raghavendra. "Spray and Wait: An Efficient Routing Scheme for Intermittently Connected Mobile Networks."
ACM SIGCOMM WDTN 2005.
Use for: your actual replication policy — reliability of epidemic at a fraction of the overhead.
Link
Burleigh, Fall, Birrane. RFC 9171 — "Bundle Protocol Version 7."
IETF, 2022.
Use for: the standardized bundle/custody format — build to this for interop & credibility.

Layer 5 Integrity, logs & CRDTs (the "web3 done right" layer)

Open-source

CRDTs — automerge/automerge, yjs/yjs · hash-linked feeds — Secure Scuttlebutt (ssbc) · content-addressed data — ipfs/kubo + IPLD

Research

Shapiro, Preguiça, Baquero, Zawirski. "Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types."
SSS 2011 (full: INRIA RR-7687, "A comprehensive study of CRDTs").
Use for: the formal basis of conflict-free partition healing. Improve: CRDT metadata growth/garbage-collection is the known cost — budget for it.
PDF
Tarr, Lavoie, Meyer, Tschudin. "Secure Scuttlebutt: An Identity-Centric Protocol for Subjective and Decentralized Applications."
ACM ICN 2019.
Use for: the closest real system to your signed hash-linked logs + gossip sync. Improve: append-only logs grow forever & single-writer — study how they handle it.
PDF
Kleppmann, Wiggins, van Hardenberg, McGranaghan. "Local-First Software: You Own Your Data, in spite of the Cloud."
ACM Onward! 2019.
Use for: the design philosophy behind "fast completeness" — instant local consistency, converge later. The manifesto for your L5.
PDF
Benet. "IPFS — Content Addressed, Versioned, P2P File System."
arXiv:1407.3561, 2014.
Use for: the content-addressing (Merkle-DAG) model for distributing cached pages/alerts by hash.
arXiv

Open-source

Research

IPTPS 2002.
Use for: the canonical statement of why identity is hard without a central authority — the threat your reputation/PoW must answer.
Link

Cross-cutting Security analyses — your "what to improve" evidence

Read these first. They are peer-reviewed teardowns of the exact class of system you're building, and the lessons are blunt: rolling your own mesh crypto fails, and even correct ciphers don't save you if the mesh/framing layer is weak. This is the most valuable feedback in the whole reference.
Albrecht, Blasco, Jensen, Mareková. "Mesh Messaging in Large-Scale Protests: Breaking Bridgefy."
CT-RSA 2021.
Lesson: Bridgefy's original design allowed impersonation, MITM, and network-wide DoS. For you: threat-model the mesh layer itself, not just message crypto.
PDF
Albrecht, Eikenberg, Paterson. "Breaking Bridgefy, again: Adopting libsignal is not enough."
USENIX Security 2022.
Lesson: even after switching to the Signal library, attacks remained via framing/parsing and mesh handling. For you: integrating a good crypto lib is necessary but not sufficient — the whole path must be hardened and independently audited.
PDF
How to use this reference: for each layer, start from the OSS project as a foundation, read the primary paper to understand the guarantee it gives, then read its "improve" note (and the Bridgefy analyses) to know where that guarantee stops. That gap — between what the component proves and what your disaster setting demands — is exactly your research and engineering agenda.
Citation note: titles, authors, and venues above are drawn from the papers' own pages and reputable indexes; verify the exact edition (conference vs. journal vs. tech-report) and check each OSS project's license and current maintenance before depending on it. Author lists are abbreviated where long.
